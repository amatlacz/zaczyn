# Symposium talk — line length study

> The single source of "why" for this project. Every agent working here reads this first.

## Goal
A 20-minute talk for the Autumn Cognitive Science Symposium (8 October 2026) presenting the finished study on line length and reading comprehension on e-readers. When this is done: an outline, timed speaker notes, and a slide deck in the institute template exist and have been rehearsed once.

## Audience
About forty peers plus the three-person symposium committee. Peers want the effect and the figure; the committee wants to see that limitations are understood. Tone: confident, plain, one message — not a defence rehearsal.

## Constraints
20 minutes + 5 for questions, slot confirmed in the programme. Institute slide template is mandatory. Results are final — no new analysis. The committee explicitly asked every speaker for a "limitations & next steps" slide.

## Glossary
- **Line length conditions:** short / medium / long — the three e-reader settings tested.
- **Perceived speed:** what readers *reported* about their pace, as opposed to measured reading speed.
- **The pilot:** an earlier, failed version of the study that led to the current design.

## Ground rules for agents
- Truth lives in this file, DECISIONS.md, and `shared/` (if present). `scratch/` is exploration — never cite it as a decision.
- Never invent a number. Every statistic comes from `sources/analysis/` or it doesn't go on a slide.
